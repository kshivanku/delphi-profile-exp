Hi Maya, I’m Ben. I’ve spent years writing, speaking, and coaching around fitness, nutrition, gut health, recovery, and performance; if you’re working on a story about how wellness advice becomes useful instead of overwhelming, I can help sort signal from noise.

Today 10:14 AM
I’m working on a story about wellness advice becoming overwhelming. Where should I start?
Start by separating advice that helps ordinary people live better from advice that mainly sells identity, fear, or optimization for its own sake.
What’s a good way to define “useful” wellness advice?
Useful advice is repeatable, affordable, and grounded in the basics: sleep, movement, food quality, stress, sunlight, relationships, and enough recovery.
How do you think about gut health without making it sound like a miracle cure?
I’d frame gut health as one signal in a larger system. Digestion, energy, mood, skin, and immune resilience can all matter, but no single supplement explains everything.
What questions should I ask people who are selling protocols?
Ask what someone can do if they have no wearable, no subscription, no lab testing, and only thirty minutes a day. That reveals whether the protocol has a practical core.
What’s the line between high performance and anxiety disguised as discipline?
Performance should create capacity. If the routine makes someone more brittle, socially isolated, or afraid of normal life, it may be control wearing the language of health.
That’s useful. What would you say to readers who feel behind?
Most people do not need a full reinvention. They need one or two boring things done consistently enough that their body starts trusting the pattern.
Which boring things would you pick first?
A consistent wake time, a walk after meals, protein at breakfast, less late-night light, and a short strength routine would outperform most complicated stacks for many people.
How should I write about supplements responsibly?
Treat supplements as context-dependent tools, not universal answers. Ask what deficiency, goal, risk, interaction, and lifestyle foundation they are supposed to address.
What’s a strong closing idea for the piece?
Health should make daily life feel more available, not more fragile. The best advice gives people agency without convincing them they are broken.
